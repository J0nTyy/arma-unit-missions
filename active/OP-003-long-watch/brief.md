# Operation Long Watch

## Situation

Syndikat has quietly reactivated the derelict airstrip at Baie de Coude on
Tanoa's northern coast. Over the past three weeks, coastal radar has tracked
seven unregistered light aircraft approaching the strip at night with
transponders dark. Regional command believes the strip is being used to move
weapons, cash, or people — but believing is not knowing, and a strike on a
civilian-adjacent airfield without hard evidence is politically impossible.

The strip sits in a bowl of dense jungle, ringed by ridgelines to the south
and east and open water to the north. Syndikat maintains a permanent
caretaker presence — estimated at eight to twelve fighters living in the old
terminal buildings — plus irregular patrols along the access road. The
locals in the fishing village of Lorio, two kilometres west, are assumed to
be sympathetic to Syndikat or at minimum unwilling to be seen talking to us.

## Background

Two months ago a cargo vessel intercepted in the Horizon Islands' territorial
waters was found carrying disassembled anti-air systems of unknown origin.
The paper trail died at a shell company registered in Georgetown. Signals
intelligence has since flagged repeated satellite-phone traffic between a
number associated with that company and a handset that geolocates, on and
off, to the Baie de Coude area.

If the airstrip is the transfer point, the anti-air systems may already be
on Tanoa. Command wants eyes on the strip across a full resupply cycle —
believed to be roughly ninety-six hours — before authorizing any kinetic
response.

## Mission

Insert covertly onto the northern coast of Tanoa, establish concealed
observation posts overlooking the Baie de Coude airstrip, document all
aircraft movements and cargo transfers across the observation window, and
exfiltrate without being detected. The mission is a strategic reconnaissance
tasking: contact with the enemy is failure, even if you win the firefight.

## Objectives

- **Primary:** Establish and maintain continuous observation of the airstrip
  for the full observation window.
- **Primary:** Photograph and log every aircraft arrival, departure, and
  cargo transfer, including tail numbers where visible.
- **Secondary:** Identify and photograph the individual coordinating
  transfers (believed to carry the flagged satellite phone).
- **Optional:** Emplace the remote listening device on the terminal
  building's landline junction box.

## Rules / Restrictions

- Weapons free **only** in self-defence after positive compromise. Any
  engagement must be reported immediately and triggers extraction planning.
- No entry into Lorio village under any circumstances.
- Radio discipline: scheduled burst transmissions only, at the top of each
  observation cycle. Continuous nets are for emergencies.
- All movement between hides happens in darkness. Daylight movement is
  command-approval only.
- No respawn: casualties must be carried and stabilized by the team.

## Equipment

Ghillie-modified fatigues, suppressed personal weapons, one designated
marksman rifle per team, spotting scopes, laser rangefinders, two long-range
cameras, one remote listening kit, and 96 hours of consumables compressed
into mission time. Each team carries a full ACE medical load — there is no
medevac inside the observation window short of mission abort.

## Intelligence

- Caretaker force: 8–12 fighters, AKs and one PKM observed, low discipline
  but local knowledge. They know the ground better than you do.
- Road patrols: a technical with 2–4 fighters drives the access road at
  irregular intervals, more frequently after aircraft arrivals.
- Dogs have been observed near the terminal. Assume they will detect an
  approach that a human would miss.
- Weather window: monsoon cells move through nightly; they mask noise and
  thermal signatures but flood the low ground east of the strip.
- The ridgeline south of the strip offers the best sightlines and the worst
  extraction options. The mangrove north shore offers the reverse. Plan the
  trade-off deliberately.

## Execution

Phase 1 — Insertion: submarine-launched boats to the mangrove coast, first
dark. Cache the boats. Move inland no more than one kilometre before
first light and lay up.

Phase 2 — Infiltration: second dark, teams move to their assigned hide
areas: OP North (mangrove fringe) and OP South (ridgeline saddle). Hides
must be complete and camouflaged before first light.

Phase 3 — Observation: maintain the log across the observation window.
Rotate observers to manage fatigue. Burst-transmit summaries on schedule.
The listening device task, if attempted, happens during the darkest
portion of the second night.

Phase 4 — Exfiltration: on order or at window close, collapse hides,
sanitize positions, and move to the boat cache. Alternate extraction is
overland to the Blue Pearl marina — eleven kilometres of jungle — and
exists so that nobody does anything desperate at the beach.

## Extraction

Primary: cached boats to the submarine rendezvous at grid 178203, third
dark. Alternate: overland to Blue Pearl marina for civilian-pattern pickup.
Emergency: flare-marked helicopter extraction at LZ Heron — understand that
using it burns the operation's deniability and will be treated as a
last resort.

## Notes for Mission Makers

The mission lives or dies on the alertness simulation: the caretaker force
escalates through four states (routine, curious, searching, alerted) driven
by noise, light, and sighting events. Test that a disciplined team can
complete the full log without ever pushing past "curious", and that a sloppy
team gets hunted. The aircraft schedule is randomized within windows so
repeat playthroughs don't memorize it. Validate the monsoon cell timing
against the observation cycles — the storms are the intended cover for hide
construction and the listening-device approach.
